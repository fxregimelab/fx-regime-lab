# Jev for the private FX desk: source review and shadow evaluation

Checked against live official sources on **2026-10-01 at 15:20 UTC**. This note evaluates a private research aid only. The desk remains a seven-pair, JPY-free manual research system. Its current source-stamped DeepSeek review is conditional; all trade forecasts remain **UNQUALIFIED**, with **zero eligible trades**. Nothing below changes those states.

## Recommendation

Test Jev as a narrow semantic control between retrieved source material and the existing DeepSeek review. The best first uses are evidence relevance, event/mechanism classification, citation support, and choosing a verbatim span from candidates found by code. Do not use Jev to write the trade thesis, calculate dates or returns, assign trade eligibility, or emit a trading probability.

This fits Jev's documented shape. It accepts state plus typed questions and returns `Noul`, `Choice`, or `Score` answers rather than generated prose ([System One](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/system-one.md), [API](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api.md)). TypeSafe says Jev performs best on narrow semantic judgments and is weak at arithmetic, date comparison, indirection, large irrelevant contexts, hostile instructions inside state, and generation ([Jev 1.13 known limits](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13.md)). Those limits match a supporting evidence-control role, not ownership of a forecast.

### Four useful jobs

1. **Evidence relevance and reranking.** Keep source retrieval deterministic. BM25, exact terms, dates, source identity, receipt time, and hashes produce a shortlist. Ask one identical Noul for each query-passage pair: “Does this passage contain evidence needed to assess this named event mechanism for this currency and 2–5-business-day horizon?” Sort by the Noul value, but retain the original shortlist and scores. TypeSafe's legal-document example raised top-1 retrieval from 5% to 18% and top-10 from 38% to 62% on its own 40-query test; this is provider evidence on another domain, not an FX result ([reranking cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/rerank_typesafe.md)). Jev cannot recover a relevant passage omitted by the first-stage retriever.

2. **Event type and directional mechanism.** Use separate questions over the same short passage. A `Choice` can label event type, for example central-bank decision/communication, inflation, labour, growth, fiscal/political, commodity/risk, positioning/flow, or `no_relevant_event`. A second `Choice`, defined for one named currency, can label the stated mechanism as supportive, adverse, mixed/conditional, no directional mechanism, or `no_match`. Include event and currency definitions in the criteria; do not rely on common shorthand. Keep observed facts separate from inferred mechanism. An event label and a direction label are research annotations, not predictions.

3. **Citation support checks.** First use exact or normalized string matching to prove that a quote appears in the captured source. Then give Jev only the claim and the surrounding section and ask whether it supports, contradicts, or says nothing about the claim. TypeSafe's cookbook uses exactly this split and sends low-confidence semantic verdicts to a human ([citation-check cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/citation_check.md)). A second model agreeing with DeepSeek is not independent evidence and does not prove correctness. The source text, deterministic quote match, label quality, and held-out error rate remain the evidence.

4. **Extraction span selection.** Split a captured article into bounded candidate sentences or spans in code. Use a `Choice` to select the span that states the claimed event, with `none` as an explicit option; copy the selected text verbatim and keep its offsets. TypeSafe recommends finding candidates first, letting Jev choose, and normalising in code. A Choice has at most 255 options, so long documents need section-first selection ([pre-parsed extraction cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/pre_parsed_value_extraction_cookbook.md)). Candidate coverage must be measured: Jev cannot select an omitted span.

## What the numbers mean

A Jev `Noul` is the model's probability that the answer to the written yes/no question is “yes.” If the question is passage relevance, `0.82` means the model assigns 0.82 to relevance under that definition. It is **not** `P(net trade wins)`, `P(TP1)`, expected return, or a confidence adjustment to the desk's price model.

Choice and Score `confidence` is derived from how concentrated their option probabilities are. Noul answers have no separate confidence field ([confidence guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence.md), [answer schema](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api.md#answer-types)). A high value can still be confidently wrong, based on missing evidence, or answer a badly framed question. Separate Nouls are not complements, and a Noul threshold cannot be carried over to an equivalent-looking Choice; TypeSafe documents failures of those structural assumptions ([known limits](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13.md)).

TypeSafe describes its model as calibrated and publishes speed and cost results. Those are vendor measurements on System One workflows. They are not verification of FX classification accuracy, causal validity, or trading performance. Likewise, DeepSeek and Jev agreement is correlated model output, not source corroboration or a consensus probability.

Jev outputs may later become **candidate research features**: for example source relevance, presence of an explicit policy mechanism, or ambiguity. The feature-discovery cookbook feeds typed judgments into a separate supervised model and keeps a final test set untouched; it specifically recommends chronological splits for future prediction and grouped splits for related rows ([AutoResearch feature discovery](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/autoresearch_feature_discovery.md)). Any use here must preserve the desk's own probability contract. Only the existing price-policy cohort, using authentic dated inputs, exact selected policy/direction/strength cells, costs, and held-out outcomes, may estimate `P(net R > 0)`. A Jev feature earns a place only if a downstream, pre-registered chronological study improves proper scoring and net results over the deterministic baseline. Its raw value never becomes that probability.

## Current product facts and prerequisites

The live model page lists version **`jev-1.13.0`**, with aliases **`jev-latest`** and **`jev-preview`** currently pointing to it. Aliases can move; a reproducible evaluation should pin the version and log the response's model ID. Listed limits are 64k total request tokens, a 32k limit for state plus the longest question, 100,000 tokens/second, 40 requests/second, text-only input, and dynamic rate limits. Price is **$0.042 per million input tokens**; output tokens are listed as free ([models and limits](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md)). These terms may change and must be captured again when an experiment starts.

The HTTP endpoint is `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone`; it requires `Authorization: Bearer <API_KEY>`. `GET /v1/models` lists account-visible aliases. The Python SDK uses `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` and provides synchronous and asynchronous clients ([API](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api.md), [Python SDK](https://docs.typesafe.ai/sdk/python.md)). Any future call must still route through `pipeline/src/ai/client.py`; the key stays server-side. This review did not inspect credentials, install the SDK, or call the paid API.

TypeSafe states that customer requests and responses are not used to train Jev, and its privacy policy says it will not train or fine-tune models on prompts or other input ([models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md), [privacy policy](https://typesafe.ai/legal/privacy-policy)). Ordinary retention is not given as a fixed duration: the policy says personal data is retained as long as reasonably necessary, while the DPA uses purpose and legal-need criteria. The docs advertise zero-data-retention for enterprise customers, not as the default ([legal index](https://docs.typesafe.ai/legal.md), [DPA](https://typesafe.ai/legal/data-processing)). Therefore the exact retention period for standard API inputs, deletion timing, account-specific usage limits, and whether this desk qualifies for ZDR remain unverified. Do not send proprietary strategy rules or unpublished trading records until those terms are confirmed for the actual account.

## Smallest defensible shadow evaluation

Freeze the question text, model ID, deterministic baseline, cost ceiling, and promotion rules before looking at the final period. Build a compact set from authentic source captures already permitted for the desk; each item keeps URL, publisher, publication/event time, receipt time, content hash, pair/currency, and span offsets. Do not use titles alone as proof of article-body support.

Have two humans label at least **240 source-event groups** spanning all seven pairs and the main event families, with disagreements adjudicated. Group all passages from the same article/event together. Use the earliest 60% for development, the next 20% for threshold selection, and the latest 20% as a sealed test, with a five-business-day embargo between periods. Report results overall and by pair, event type, source, and no-match class; small cohorts stay explicitly inconclusive.

Compare Jev with deterministic baselines: BM25 order for relevance, rules/keyword maps for event type, exact quote matching for quote presence, and sentence/section heuristics for span selection. Measure Recall@5 and nDCG@5 for retrieval; macro-F1 and per-class precision/recall for classifications; exact-span overlap and `none` accuracy for extraction; and support/contradiction/unsupported recall for citation checks. Also record abstention rate, human-review rate, p50/p95 latency, input tokens, realised cost, API errors, and answer drift on a fixed replay set.

Adversarial cases must include negation, quoted third-party views, conditional statements, stale versus current claims, conflicting passages, near-duplicate events, currency-base/quote reversals, EURGBP cross effects, missing evidence, injected instructions in source text, and passages that name a currency without a causal mechanism. Arithmetic, date ordering, source clocks, hashes, candidate construction, and permission checks remain deterministic.

Pre-register promotion only to an **assistive shadow role** if the sealed test shows: Recall@5 improves by at least 10 percentage points over BM25 without lowering passage coverage; macro-F1 is at least 0.80; `no_match` recall and citation-error recall are each at least 0.90; every selected span is byte-linked to the captured source; no adversarial item bypasses review; and measured cost and p95 latency fit the agreed budget. Use bootstrap confidence intervals and require the lower bound of the retrieval gain to remain above zero. Failed API calls, uncertain judgments, or changed source hashes must fall back to the deterministic path and human review.

Even after those gates, Jev remains outside trade eligibility and probability production. A later feature study needs its own grouped chronological holdout, overlap/embargo controls, calibration evidence, and net-after-cost comparison against the frozen price-model baseline. Until those results exist, the honest decision is **shadow-test Jev for evidence quality; do not claim strategy improvement**.
