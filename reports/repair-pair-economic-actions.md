## EURUSD: DXY orientation and remaining macro assumptions

**There is no DXY confirmation leg in the current EURUSD special function.** `special.py:124–150` uses only Bund–BTP and ECB assets; DXY is consumed in the INR branch at lines 166–178. The scheduler's `orchestrator.py:230–257,295–299` uses the **absolute** overnight DXY move for operational stress. That magnitude is deliberately directionless and must not be reused as dollar-up news. Thus the current defect is missing EURUSD confirmation plus an inverted macro output, not an existing EURUSD DXY expression that merely needs a minus sign.

For a separately registered DXY directional candidate, positive dollar-basket movement has negative EURUSD orientation. ICE's formula includes `EURUSD^-0.576`. The euro weight also makes DXY partly a transformation of the same EURUSD price being studied; do not count it as independent corroboration or causal dollar news. Freeze return interval and cutoff. A high DXY **level percentile** is not the same observation as DXY rising. [ICE USDX formula and weights, page 2](https://www.theice.com/publicdocs/futures_us/ICE_Dollar_Index_FAQ.pdf).

`fetch_bund_btp_spread` at `pipeline/src/fetchers/yields.py:174–199` returns Germany minus Italy in percentage points. A change from −1 to −2 means the Italy-over-Germany spread widened from 1 to 2 pp. Keeping this raw convention is valid; converting to Italy-minus-Germany and also negating its interpretation would double-invert it. The current scalar mapping `(spread + 0.5)/2.5` is an unvalidated level rule. A widening/spread-change candidate must be a different feature, with matched observation dates and source frequency.

The ECB millions-to-billions repair is already present at `yields.py:148–168`; do not divide again. FRED identifies `ECBASSETSW` as weekly, in millions of euros. The outstanding issue is `_scalar_eur_ecb` at `special.py:93–98`: `(assets_bn−6000)/3000` treats high assets as easing and dollar strength. Asset level is not an announcement surprise. Preserve asset level as descriptive context; any relative ECB-versus-Fed change, balance-sheet composition or surprise candidate needs a new definition and test. [FRED/ECB series metadata](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ECBASSETSW).

Finally, `pipeline/src/logic/layer1_gate.py:75–95` maps a positive non-INR tier to USD-strength labels for **both** EURUSD and USDJPY. After adopting pair-up direction, positive EURUSD must not be described as dollar strength. `run_layer1_gate:183–189` also uses absolute rate/inflation deviations to produce `USD_POLICY_BREAKOUT`; that is neither a directional nor an identified policy-shock measurement. Repair labels and their `prior_tier_from_regime` round trip together. Keep descriptive state separate from the published directional call.

## Rates: preserve completed repairs; split three remaining quantities

`pipeline/src/signals/rate_inputs.py:52–91`, `two_year_spread`, already enforces matched two-year government-yield definitions, currency order and percent units. `real_yield_spread:113–126` already requires both inflation legs; current missing foreign inflation remains unavailable. `rate.py:48–104` excludes unqualified old history. The default configured foreign instruments still fail qualification, so no working matched daily local two-year feed is established by these repairs.

The remaining semantic error is `rate_direction_from_spreads` at `rate.py:133–160`. Its comment says the normalized score detects changes, but `_mad_z_from_series:163–181` measures the current value's distance from a historical median. In the scheduler, that current value is **yield spread divided by volatility**, not spread change (`orchestrator.py:1325–1355`). A falling volatility denominator can change the rate score with no rate movement at all.

Use distinct fields and transformations:

1. `spread_level_pp = yield_base − yield_quote`: descriptive rate advantage, using qualified instruments.
2. `spread_change_pp = spread_level_pp[t] − spread_level_pp[t−k]`: repricing over a frozen interval with compatible observations. It is not automatically unexpected news.
3. `policy_surprise`: matched expected-policy-path revision around a timestamped decision relative to prior expectations. Leave unavailable without verified expectation data; neither level rank nor realized yield change may fill it.

The Federal Reserve's event study uses unexpected changes in relative expected rates around announcements, and distinguishes them from anticipated policy actions. It supports a news hypothesis, not the present carry-level z-score's future accuracy. A positive US-relative surprise maps negatively to EURUSD and positively to USDJPY as a candidate orientation; post-publication persistence must be tested separately. [Federal Reserve, 22 September 2017](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/ifdp-notes/the-sensitivity-of-the-us-dollar-exchange-rate-to-changes-in-monetary-policy-expectations-20170922.htm).

`compute_risk_adjusted_carry_v2:246–271` can select IV today while `build_carry_history_from_rows:48–73` constructs an RV20-only reference. Require the same denominator family, annualization and units across current/history. `fetch_implied_vol` at `pipeline/src/fetchers/volatility.py:33–46` currently returns `None` for every pair, so the denominator-switch defect is dormant, not evidence of a current IV feed. Do not describe EVZ/JYVIX as wired in. Percent/percent and decimal/decimal ratios should agree; mixed units must be rejected by metadata validation.

## USDJPY: orientation is not a contrarian rule; intervention is not a price target

CME yen futures are quoted in USD per JPY, the inverse orientation of USDJPY spot. Therefore a positive signed long-JPY exposure becomes negative signed USDJPY exposure. If a separately registered contrarian rule reverses that exposure, its forecast becomes positive USDJPY. Those are **two different operations**. Using the original positive JPY score directly in a USDJPY composite accidentally resembles a contrarian prediction without declaring one. [CME yen contract, Chapter 253](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/CME/III/250/253/253.pdf).

CFTC's Asset Manager and Leveraged Funds categories describe business roles, not demonstrated forecasting skill. `compute_cot_smart_spread_percentile` at `cot.py:61–109` mixes AM-minus-LM values with plain net-long fallback rows at lines 93–101; those are different variables. Stop that splice. Missing either category means that observation lacks the AM-minus-LM feature. Keep Legacy non-commercial, TFF asset-manager and TFF leveraged-fund series separately identified. ## USDINR: retain unknown intervention state

The current INR special score is entirely oil and DXY. Algebraically, `special.py:177–178` simplifies to `0.525*n_oil + 0.475*n_dxy`; the “EM” term adds no independent information. It contains no reserve data, spot-fixing deviation, actual RBI operation or estimated intervention probability. Rename the inputs honestly and leave RBI intervention state unknown. Do not infer Active Suppression, Passive or Allowing Depreciation merely from this sum, low volatility or a small spot move.

RBI describes two-sided intervention aimed at limiting excessive volatility and maintaining orderly conditions without a particular exchange-rate target. Reserve changes also include valuation changes and income. Neither policy description nor reserve level proves a daily intervention. The identity's “dominant driver” premise therefore requires research evidence for the modeled period; this audit does not establish it. [RBI November 2024 Bulletin, Box 2](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=22995), [RBI reserve explanation](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?Id=18086).

Separate transactions from valuation and stock from flow. Choose a label period supported by disclosures; do not interpolate monthly operations into invented daily intervention events. A rise in reserves with no known transaction breakdown must leave intervention direction unknown. Fixing-deviation and volatility-suppression proxies remain candidates, with alternative explanations and missing-data states.

The oil fetcher uses Yahoo `CL=F`, including `pipeline/src/fetchers/cross_asset.py:220–223`, while the project architecture calls for Brent. Treat this as a WTI futures proxy with contract/roll conventions, not Brent or India's import basket. Oil import exposure justifies a candidate, not an unconditional forecast sign. [RBI oil-supply-news study](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=21864). Verify source identity before a replacement; no market-data acquisition occurred here.
